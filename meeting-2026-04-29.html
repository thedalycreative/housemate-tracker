Zebra Jukebox House Agreement

29 April 2026

💬 Communication

  1. iMessage is for house matters only - bills, property, dogs, household stuff. One topic at a time.
  2. Messenger is for casual banter, jokes, Scrabble, life chat.
  3. Emojis are required in house chat to indicate tone. Nessa cannot read body language or facial expressions; emojis tell her if a message is serious, joking, or sarcastic.
  4. 24-hour cool-off rule when emotionally charged - but preface it ("I need 24 hours" / "I need space"). Silence without warning gets misread.
  5. If a chat is unreadable (broken keyboard, no interpreter), do not voice-dictate a flood of messages the others can't follow up on.

🧹 Cleaning & Home

  1. Cleaning is split by ability, not by day. Nessa washes and dries dishes, puts away, does her own laundry. The others handle vacuuming and anything that needs eyesight.
  2. Tim will research a fortnightly professional cleaner, split three ways.
  3. Google Home camera set up before any cleaner visits - past theft has made trust hard.
  4. Lock doors. Block the cellar opening behind the red couch. Report the next-door bull mastiff to the council ranger if it lunges again.

📅 Meetings & Tools

  1. Weekly house meetings on Tuesdays at 6:30 pm with an Auslan interpreter booked. Cancel with at least 2 days' notice or Nessa loses funding.
  2. First meeting after this one: Tuesday 19 May 2026 (Nessa back from Bali on 12 May).
  3. Shared Google Calendar for house events, support workers, away dates.
  4. Shared spreadsheet for rent, power, bills - on this Zebra Jukebox site, with Google Sheets as backup.

🏠 Future

📑 Summary

The first formal house meeting for the Zebra Jukebox household. The trigger was a flare-up the night before - Nessa's keyboard broke mid-conversation, voice-to-text went sideways, and a stack of unanswered messages landed on Tim and D'Arcy at the same time. Everyone showed up wanting to repair, not just rehash.

The bulk of the conversation was about communication channels (separating house chat from banter, requiring emojis for tone, naming a cool-off period before retreating), cleaning (splitting tasks by ability rather than day, agreeing to research a professional cleaner), and cadence (weekly meetings on Tuesday with an interpreter, starting 19 May once Nessa is back from Bali).

Two pieces of new information surfaced: there is a cellar opening behind the red couch the previous tenant never fully closed, and D'Arcy's bag was stolen from his car the same day after a threatening student incident at his campus. Both reinforced the security thread already running through the conversation.

The meeting also re-set the medium-term plan: Tim and D'Arcy are looking at relocating for work between June and August 2026, and Nessa will start her own housing search once she's back. The group explicitly agreed that no one will be left "high and dry."

💡 Communication Style & Hints

Things that worked, and small adjustments that will probably help next time. Written as observations, not judgments.

✅ What's working

  • You all sat through 2+ hours with an interpreter - that's a real commitment, not a token meeting.
  • Concrete agreements were reached on every topic raised. Nothing was left to "we'll see."
  • Humour is doing real work - the taco-Tuesday/toilet-Tuesday confusion, "awkward turtle," the bikini cheerleader joke - tension dropped every time someone laughed.
  • Nessa offered emojis as a literal accessibility need, and it was accepted instantly without it being made a big deal of.
  • D'Arcy named the retreat-vs-engage difference between his style and Nessa's. Naming it is most of the fix.
  • Nessa explicitly offered to plan around Tim & D'Arcy's work timing, not the reverse. That's generous.

💡 Small adjustments

  • Tim often carries the systems / tech / "let me build a tool" load. That's a strength but it can also become "Tim solves it alone." Check in before adding to your own plate.
  • D'Arcy tends to land somewhere useful but takes the long way. The others can resist the urge to finish his sentences - the wandering is part of the thinking.
  • Nessa sometimes defaults to explaining what her disability prevents when challenged. Try framing requests as "let's try this together and see" - it gives her room to opt in rather than defend.
  • Watch the back-to-back-message pattern. When 4-5 unread messages stack up on the same topic, the original point gets buried. One message, then wait.
  • If voice-to-text or the keyboard isn't working, fall back to physical signals - hand gestures, charades, written notes. Tim has said he reads these well.
  • The 24-hour rule only works if it's named out loud. "I need space" before going quiet, every time.

🔥 Hot tip for next meeting

The structure ChatGPT suggested (take turns, one topic at a time, park or resolve before moving on) actually held the room together. Keep it. Open the next meeting by re-stating those four ground rules out loud - it takes 30 seconds and re-anchors everyone.
